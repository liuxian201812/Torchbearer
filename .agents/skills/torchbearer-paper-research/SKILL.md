# Retrieve scientific source evidence

Carry out source investigations under the coordinator's management to support
the researcher's current scientific question.

Resolve the paper's DOI or canonical identity and check the existing source
collection first. Search official publication records, open repositories and
authorized institutional services through the available acquisition tools.
Accept user-provided full text with its source and version recorded.

Match the retrieved material to the requested title, authors and publication
identity. Keep versions explicit when a preprint and published article differ.
Read the relevant full-text sections and retain precise evidence locations;
use metadata and abstracts to select sources.

For a precedent that affects the research direction or contribution assessment,
prepare a [contribution card](references/contribution-cards.md). Retain the source
and evidence, problem and context, scientific contribution and value, and its
relation to the current project. Attribute established content to the source
and state the project's additional result under its actual assumptions. Reuse
the paper's stable card identity when adding a new comparison.

Send the coordinator the source identity, publication version, provenance,
evidence passages or locations, and the scientific question they address.
Preserve separate identities for supplementary material and related papers.

If a required source remains unavailable, send the coordinator a concrete
material request with its identity and scientific purpose. Continue work whose
evidence dependencies are already satisfied, then resume the affected step
when the source becomes available.
